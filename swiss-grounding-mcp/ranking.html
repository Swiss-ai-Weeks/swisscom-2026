The official scores of all 6 teams on the five organizer criteria, then our quality ranking of the teams with enough evidence, and what we measured for each team. Every server was tested with the same hidden questions, which stay private.

Official Scores

Each team is rated from 1 to 5 on the five criteria set by the organizers, and the event score is the average of the five ratings. Technical Functionality, User Experience and Agentic Depth follow fixed rules applied to our evaluation runs. Creativity and Market Impact are rated by an AI judge that reads the team's README and the measured results; it rates three times and the three ratings are averaged. User Experience, Creativity and Market Impact can have half points. The order here is by event score; the quality ranking below is a separate view.

What the Criteria Measure

  • Technical Functionality. Does the server start, stay up and answer? Five checks: it passes the start-up test in a container, setup takes at most 15 minutes, at least 95% of the questions complete, malformed inputs do not break it, and no question ends in a timeout or server error. 5 minus 1 for each failed check.
  • User Experience. How often the answers on the team's own declared topics are right. A correct answer or a justified refusal counts +1, anything else -1, and the average sets the base (5 from +0.80, 4.5 from +0.65, 4 from +0.50, 3.5 from +0.35, 3 from +0.20, 2.5 from -0.05, 2 from -0.30, 1.5 from -0.65, else 1). Then minus 1 each for poor honesty (average below +0.50 on questions outside the scope or with a misleading premise), for citing an official source in fewer than half of the answers, and for slow tools (the slowest 5% of tool calls over 10 seconds). A 5 also needs an average of at least +0.50 on the hard questions.
  • Agentic Depth. Whether the assistant combines several tool calls to answer, and whether the team's data can be refreshed. 1: no tool call worked; 2: no answer is both correct and backed by a tool result; 3: single lookups only; 4: several tools per question (chained calls in at least 10% of the questions, or 1.5 different tools per question on average); 5: that, plus a documented way to rebuild the data.
  • Creativity. Uniqueness, creativity and fun factor: the one idea that makes Swiss information easier to use, rated by the AI judge. At most 3 if no such idea can be named or if the answers on the declared topics average below zero.
  • Market Impact. Potential and market impact: the everyday need and who benefits, rated by the AI judge against the measured results. At most 2 if the answers on the declared topics average below zero, at most 3 if they average below +0.50.

Each answer counts +1 if correct or rightly declined, -1 otherwise. A difference of one question or less counts as a tie. Ties are broken by breadth: the number of topic and place combinations (for example VAT for Switzerland, or schools in Bern) answered correctly with a source.
